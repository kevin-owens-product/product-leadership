**COLE:** Harborline ended Q7 at one hundred twenty-eight point five million of ARR. During Q8, new-logo ARR added three point eight million. Core expansion added two point seven. Assist expansion added three point five. Churn and contraction removed two point six. Net addition: seven point four. Ending ARR: one hundred thirty-five point nine.

**ASHA:** Now separate the acquired cohort. Harborline's organic business entered Q8 with one hundred sixteen point five million. It added three point eight of new logos, two point three of core expansion, three point two of AI expansion, and lost one point nine to churn and contraction. **COLE:** Beacon entered with twelve million. It lost seven hundred thousand, added four hundred thousand of core expansion and three hundred thousand of AI expansion, and ended at twelve million. ### WHY A BUYER RE-TRADES

**ASHA:** A re-trade is not always bad faith. A buyer prices uncertainty. If diligence reveals a fact outside the agreed assumptions, price should move. If the fact was already disclosed and the buyer simply wants to use process fatigue, the seller needs an alternative and a walk-away point.

**COLE:** The first job is therefore to classify every objection. New fact, known risk, definitional difference, missing evidence, or negotiating position.

**ASHA:** New fact can change price. Known risk should already be in the bid. A definitional difference needs reconciliation. Missing evidence is the seller's problem until supplied. A negotiating position needs commercial resolve, not another spreadsheet.

**COLE:** The buyer has six major objections. AI revenue quality. Beacon retention. EBITDA adjustments. Unrealized integration benefits. Security and data rights. And the claim that Harborline's growth quality deserves a strategic multiple.

**ASHA:** We will take them one at a time. Not to win an argument. To determine whether eight point two five is supported.

### OBJECTION ONE: IS AI ARR REALLY ARR?

**COLE:** Assist has ten point nine million dollars of ARR at Q8. The buyer says the product is young, usage based, and partly bundled. It proposes treating a portion as experimental revenue.

**ASHA:** Harborline starts with its metric contract. The ten point nine million includes annual minimum workflow commitments in active customer contracts. It excludes uncommitted overage forecasts. It excludes pilots without a production commitment. It excludes services. It excludes contracts waiting for activation.

**COLE:** Then the contract-to-cash chain. For each account: signed order, effective date, minimum commitment, invoice, collection, activation event, usage, renewal date, expansion or contraction. The buyer can select a sample and trace it end to end.

**ASHA:** That proves the accounting quality. It does not prove durability.

**COLE:** Durability needs product evidence. Recommendation acceptance rose from forty-four percent in pilot to seventy-six percent in Q8. Confirmed severe errors fell from four point eight per thousand assisted jobs to zero point four. All-in variable cost fell from two dollars eighty per job to sixty-eight cents against an average realized price of four dollars.

**ASHA:** Which means workload contribution before allocated product and support cost rose from thirty percent to eighty-three percent. Harborline also shows the fully loaded product gross margin, which is lower because it includes dedicated reliability, customer support, and reserved vendor capacity. It does not present variable inference margin as company gross margin.

**COLE:** The buyer asks the right question: are customers consuming what they committed to buy? Harborline supplies the distribution, not the average. Accounts are grouped by percent of committed workflow volume used, time since activation, vertical, implementation partner, and renewal status. Shelfware is visible.

**ASHA:** The first renewal cohort is small. Harborline says so. It began with one point two million of Assist ARR. It retained one point one three million before expansion, about ninety-four percent GRR, then expanded to one point three five million. Encouraging, not conclusive. The remaining ten million-plus has activation and usage evidence but not a full annual renewal cycle.

**COLE:** That admission strengthens the defense. The company is not asking the buyer to mistake early evidence for mature evidence. It is showing why the uncertainty is already bounded through commitments, activation, usage, customer outcomes, and the first renewal cohort.

**ASHA:** Model dependence is next. One external model currently handles fifty-eight percent of assisted jobs. Harborline provides the routing policy, vendor terms, cost history, failure procedures, and shadow evaluations against an alternative model. The alternative is modestly more expensive and slightly lower on acceptance, but remains inside the service guardrail.

**COLE:** That does not make models interchangeable. It proves Harborline owns more than a prompt tied to one vendor. It owns identity, permissions, retrieval, workflow, evaluations, customer integration, and the workload profit and loss. The outside model is important and replaceable with a measurable cost.

**ASHA:** The defensible claim is not "our AI cannot be copied." It is "our service is contracted, active, improving economically, embedded in a workflow, and portable enough that vendor concentration is managed." That claim can survive diligence.

### OBJECTION TWO: DID BEACON CREATE VALUE?

**ASHA:** The buyer's next point is blunt. Harborline paid forty-two million dollars for twelve million of Beacon ARR. Two quarters later, Beacon still has twelve million. Where is the value?

**COLE:** Start by refusing the wrong answer. Harborline does not say, "Beacon added twelve million of ARR." It purchased twelve million. The value case is retention, customer access, workflow completion, verified savings, and future expansion supported by evidence.

**ASHA:** The acquired bridge is clean. Opening ARR twelve million. Churn and contraction negative seven hundred thousand. Core expansion positive four hundred thousand. Assist expansion positive three hundred thousand. Ending ARR twelve million.

**COLE:** The buyer focuses on the gross loss. Harborline supplies the account list, churn reasons, and renewal calendar. The weak industrial cross-sell cohort from last episode remains removed from forecast. There is no claim that all one hundred eighty Beacon customers are ready Harborline cross-sell targets.

**ASHA:** Only a portion of Beacon ARR has reached renewal since close. In the due cohort, gross retention is ninety-four percent. That is better than Beacon's pre-close ninety-one percent GRR, but too early to declare a trend. The unrenewed cohort is shown by renewal date and risk rating rather than treated as proven retention.

**COLE:** Product integration evidence shows the inspect-to-remediate workflow operating in the controlled fire-safety cohort. Identity and permissions are mapped. Inspection defects can open a remediation quote without re-keying. Assist can retrieve the relevant asset history within the authorized tenant. Customer administration is converging. The codebases have not been forced together.

**ASHA:** Cost synergy is also separated into realized and forecast. One point two million of run-rate cost has been removed and validated by finance. The original eighteen-month target was three million. Harborline claims one point two today and presents the remaining initiatives with cost, owner, timing, and dependency. It does not add one point eight million to current EBITDA.

**COLE:** The buyer says the integration is incomplete. Harborline agrees. Incomplete is not the same as failed. The evidence shows what is unified, what remains separate, why it remains separate, what customers experienced, and what benefit has reached financial results.

**ASHA:** If Harborline had merged the codebases and fired the team in one hundred days, the integration slide might look more complete. The customer and security evidence would likely look worse. Exit defense rewards disciplined incompleteness when the alternative was irreversible damage.

### OBJECTION THREE: WHICH EBITDA IS REAL?

**ASHA:** Buyers do not pay for a seller's adjective before EBITDA. Adjusted. Run-rate. Pro forma. Synergized. Each can be legitimate. Together they can become fiction.

**COLE:** Harborline's year-two operating ledger shows thirty-five point zero five million of EBITDA and a twenty-eight point four percent margin. Q8 shows eleven point one five million and thirty-two percent. No future Beacon synergy is included in those operating results.

**ASHA:** The evidence room begins with the general ledger and walks to management reporting. Revenue classification, hosting and model costs, support, implementation, research and development, sales commissions, restructuring, transaction cost, and integration expense each have a policy. Every adjustment has an invoice or payroll source, a stated rationale, and a recurrence test.

**COLE:** Recurrence is the key question. A legal fee to acquire Beacon is transaction-specific. A cloud bill labeled "temporary integration" that will persist for eighteen months is an operating cost during those eighteen months. A role the company plans to remove is not a realized saving while the person remains employed.

**ASHA:** Harborline refuses to defend the price by annualizing Q8 and adding the remaining Beacon plan. It presents the actual thirty-five point zero five million, the current exit rate, and a separate initiative ledger. The buyer can assign its own probability to the future.

**COLE:** That discipline matters even though the offer is expressed as an ARR multiple. EBITDA quality affects debt capacity, cash conversion, and the multiple a buyer is willing to pay. If growth consumes cash indefinitely, ARR is less valuable.

**ASHA:** Net debt also receives a contract, not a last-minute debate. Debt instruments, accrued interest, cash, letters of credit, capital leases, and debt-like items are reconciled to a specified date. The canonical outcome uses one hundred eighty-seven point nine million of net debt. If the definition changes, equity proceeds change dollar for dollar.

### OBJECTION FOUR: SECURITY AND DATA RIGHTS

**COLE:** The buyer describes the cross-tenant retrieval near miss as evidence that Assist cannot be safely scaled.

**ASHA:** Harborline does not minimize it. The evidence index contains the original detection, affected systems, access path, customer communication, pause decision, remediation, tenant-aware retrieval design, regression tests, independent review, and every later exception. The buyer sees the control improve because the incident happened.

**COLE:** A clean incident record is not a record with no incidents. It is a record where severity, exposure, response, learning, and recurrence are knowable. Hiding the near miss would turn a contained product risk into a transaction-integrity risk.

**ASHA:** Data rights require equal precision. Every Assist customer grants the processing rights required to deliver its tenant-contained service. Contracts covering most, but not all, usage also permit defined de-identified product improvement. Data without those broader rights remains excluded from pooled improvement.

**COLE:** Beacon inspection photographs have their own schedule because subcontractor origin creates ambiguity. Harborline can retrieve authorized evidence for the customer's workflow. It does not claim unrestricted ownership of the historical image corpus. The evidence room maps dataset, source, tenant, purpose, jurisdiction, retention, and permitted use.

**ASHA:** The buyer wanted a universal data moat. Harborline offers something more defensible: contractual access to serve customers, workflow context, permission-aware retrieval, improving evaluations, and an explicit boundary around what cannot be reused.

**COLE:** That may be worth less than an imaginary right to train on everything. It is worth more than discovering after close that the imaginary right never existed.

### THE EVIDENCE ROOM

**ASHA:** Exit readiness did not begin when the offer arrived. It began in episode two, when Harborline rebased the metrics. The evidence room is the ownership period made inspectable.

**COLE:** Section one is revenue quality. Customer contract, amendment, activation, invoice, cash, product usage, renewal, and ARR classification. The organic and acquired bridges reconcile quarter by quarter. Bookings, billings, revenue, ARR, and cash remain separate.

**ASHA:** Section two is cohort economics. GRR and NRR by start period, vertical, ACV, product set, implementation path, and acquisition source. Concentration, downgrades, concessions, and shelfware are visible. No blended average gets to hide the weak cohort.

**COLE:** Section three is Assist durability. Workload volume, successful outcomes, recommendation acceptance, severe-error rate, human-review rate, latency, model mix, variable cost, fully loaded product gross margin, customer activation, commitment consumption, renewal, and expansion.

**ASHA:** Section four is product, architecture, security, and data rights. System map, identity boundaries, reliability history, incident register, remediation evidence, vendor exposure, model-switch test, dataset-rights matrix, retention policy, and independent assurance.

**COLE:** Section five is Beacon. Entry quality of earnings, purchase accounting, acquired ARR bridge, renewal schedule, customer evidence, people retention, Day One and Day One Hundred work, product-integration proof, cost synergy ledger, revenue-synergy funnel, and every variance from the acquisition case.

**ASHA:** Section six is financial quality. General-ledger tie-out, revenue recognition, gross-margin anatomy, EBITDA policy, adjustment schedule, cash conversion, working capital, capitalized development, debt agreements, covenant calculations, and net-debt schedule.

**COLE:** Each item has an owner, reporting period, source system, reviewer, and refresh date. A folder full of screenshots is not an evidence room. Evidence has lineage.

**ASHA:** And access is staged. Commercially sensitive customer detail appears after the appropriate gate. Personal data is minimized. The buyer receives enough to verify without Harborline creating a new security incident in diligence.

### HOW MANAGEMENT DEFENDS WITHOUT SELLING A FANTASY

**COLE:** The management presentation has eight moves.

**ASHA:** First, customer workflow. What contractors need to accomplish and where Harborline produces measurable value. Not the total addressable market in the first five slides.

**COLE:** Second, the verified Q0 baseline. Ninety-six million ARR, one hundred one percent NRR, twenty-six million EBITDA, and the problems Harborline inherited. Showing the unflattering starting point makes the improvement auditable.

**ASHA:** Third, the organic value bridge. Price, new logos, core expansion, Assist expansion, churn, and contraction. Quarter by quarter. No acquisition mixed into the organic story.

**COLE:** Fourth, Assist as a production service. Customer job, service envelope, quality, cost, risk, activation, renewal, and vendor portability. No generic AI-market slide.

**ASHA:** Fifth, Beacon. What was bought, what was retained, what was lost, what has integrated, what has not, which synergies are realized, and why the remaining plan is credible.

**COLE:** Sixth, operating leverage. Revenue and gross-margin drivers, current quarter versus trailing year, cash conversion, and the organization that sustains the cadence.

**ASHA:** Seventh, risks. Model dependence, contract rights, integration, customer concentration, leverage, and talent. A seller who cannot articulate the risks invites the buyer to price them at the maximum.

**COLE:** Eighth, the next owner's value-creation map. Not booked into Harborline's history and not required to defend current performance. A transparent set of opportunities the strategic buyer may value differently because it owns adjacent assets.

**ASHA:** That last distinction protects credibility. Harborline proves what it created. The buyer decides what unique synergies it can create. Harborline does not sneak buyer-specific value into adjusted EBITDA and then multiply it.

**ASHA:** Three paths, Kevin.

Harborline would re-lever for a small check.

**COLE:** Path three: defend eight point two five and be willing to hold if the buyer will not pay for the verified quality. At eight point two five, enterprise value is one billion one hundred twenty-one point two million. Less debt, equity proceeds are nine hundred thirty-three point three million. Two point two two times MOIC. Forty-nine point one percent two-year IRR.

**COLE:** Grade the choices. Value created: the hold case has the highest forecast MOIC; the original offer has the highest realized annualized return. Cash consumed: selling ends the exposure; holding continues debt service and investment. Risk added: hold carries the most, accepting seven carries the least. Speed to evidence: a sale closes the ledger now; a hold creates more evidence over time. Reversibility: accepting a sale is irreversible, but so is letting a strategic premium disappear while you test another three years.

**ASHA:** Decide. Accept the re-trade, hold or recapitalize, or defend the original price with a real willingness to walk. Write down the evidence that justifies your reservation price.

### THE CANONICAL CALL

**ASHA:** Defend the original eight point two five times. Do not defend every management claim. Defend the price with the narrower set of claims Harborline can prove.

**COLE:** Contracted and active AI ARR is ten point nine million. Early renewal evidence is promising and limited. Beacon's acquired cohort ends at twelve million through seven hundred thousand of loss offset by seven hundred thousand of expansion. One point two million of Beacon cost synergy is realized. The remaining one point eight is not. The earlier operating-system redesign has separately delivered its three point four million net annualized run rate, with no Beacon synergy inside it. Organic ARR is one hundred twenty-three point nine million, up twenty-two point one percent. Year-two EBITDA is thirty-five point zero five million. Q8 margin is thirty-two percent. Both are presented correctly.

**ASHA:** Capital: no new product or transformation budget. Harborline uses only the already approved sale-process reserve and accepts continued exposure to one hundred eighty-seven point nine million of net debt if it walks. Any new management incentive, customer concession, or roadmap commitment returns to the board rather than hiding inside the defense.

**ASHA:** The decision memo assigns the CFO to own the financial and return bridge, the product and technology leader to own product, AI, architecture, and workload proof, the general counsel to own contract and data-rights lineage, and the CEO to own the integrated management case.

**COLE:** Target: restore the eight point two five offer without adding unsupported adjustments or forward synergies. Guardrails: customer operations do not slow for diligence, security controls do not weaken for data-room speed, and management does not make roadmap promises to close the deal.

**ASHA:** Reopen the valuation internally if three things fail verification: contract-level ARR differs by more than one percent, the Beacon due-renewal cohort falls below ninety percent GRR, or a material data-rights gap prevents the current Assist service. A price defense that cannot survive its own kill criteria is bluffing.

**COLE:** Harborline also establishes a negotiated floor below the original offer and above the re-trade, supported by the risk-adjusted hold case. The exact reservation price stays with the board. The operating team does not leak it through anxious concessions.

### WHAT CHANGED THE BUYER'S PRICE

**ASHA:** Over ten days, the evidence defeats the assumptions behind the re-trade. Contract-level ARR ties within four tenths of one percent. The organic and acquired bridges reconcile to billing and the ledger. Assist's active contracts, workload contribution, early renewals, tenant-isolation controls, and successful model-switch test establish a production service rather than a thin feature. Beacon's retained cohort and realized savings tie to named contracts and removed cost.

**COLE:** Harborline does not win every point. The buyer still discounts Assist for its short renewal history, excludes the remaining one point eight million of Beacon synergy, and assigns no value to historical data whose rights remain restricted. Those uncertainties stay visible in the price discussion instead of disappearing into an adjusted metric.

**ASHA:** The strategic rationale supplies the final distinction. This buyer already owns distribution and workflow assets adjacent to Harborline's contractors. It can create value from the inspect-to-remediate network that Harborline cannot book on its own. Management shows the verified platform and refuses to claim the buyer's synergy; the buyer chooses to pay for that strategic option rather than let Harborline pursue the credible hold case.

**COLE:** The restored offer is therefore a negotiation consequence, not a narrative reward. Harborline proves the risks were narrower than the re-trade assumed, concedes the risks that remain, and keeps a real walk-away alternative. Evidence changes the buyer's risk adjustment. Strategic fit supports the premium.

### THE RETURN BRIDGE

**ASHA:** The buyer restores the eight point two five price, subject to the verified net-debt schedule. Now do the final math without skipping a line.

**COLE:** Enterprise value equals ending ARR multiplied by the offer multiple. One hundred thirty-five point nine million times eight point two five equals one billion one hundred twenty-one point one seven five million dollars, rounded to one point one two one billion.

**ASHA:** Enterprise value belongs to all capital providers. To reach sponsor equity value, subtract net debt of one hundred eighty-seven point nine million. Equity proceeds are nine hundred thirty-three point two seven five million, rounded to nine hundred thirty-three point three.

**COLE:** The sponsor invested four hundred twenty million at entry. Nine hundred thirty-three point three divided by four hundred twenty equals two point two two times gross MOIC.

**ASHA:** The holding period is two years and there were no sponsor distributions in this simplified return. Two-year annualized IRR is the square root of two point two two, minus one. Approximately forty-nine point one percent.

**COLE:** Those are gross sponsor returns before transaction fees, carried interest, and fund-level effects. The same convention was used in the underwrite, so the comparison is coherent.

**ASHA:** Now the sensitivity. At seven times, enterprise value is nine hundred fifty-one point three million, equity is seven hundred sixty-three point four, MOIC is one point eight two, and IRR is thirty-four point eight percent. At seven and a half times, equity is about eight hundred thirty-one point four, MOIC is one point nine eight, and IRR is forty point seven. At seven point seven five, equity is about eight hundred sixty-five point three, MOIC is two point zero six, and IRR is forty-three point five. At eight point two five, equity is nine hundred thirty-three point three, MOIC two point two two, IRR forty-nine point one.

**COLE:** One turn of ARR at exit is one hundred thirty-five point nine million of enterprise value. That is why revenue quality, architecture, security, and data rights become financial topics. They affect the confidence with which a buyer applies a multiple to the recurring base.

### THE FINAL OPERATOR DRILL

**ASHA:** Your final artifact has four parts: an exit memo, an evidence index, a management-presentation outline, and a return sensitivity.

**COLE:** The exit memo answers five questions. Why sell now? What is verified? What remains uncertain? What price does the evidence support? What would make holding superior? It includes buyer universe, process risk, management incentives, tax and transaction considerations, and the board's reservation logic.

**ASHA:** The evidence index is not a list of file names. For every claim, record the source system, definition, period, owner, reviewer, refresh date, and buyer access level. Prioritize revenue, retention, AI workload economics, data rights, security, Beacon integration, EBITDA, cash, and debt.

**COLE:** The management outline follows the eight moves we described: customer workflow, verified baseline, organic bridge, production AI proof, acquisition evidence, operating leverage, risks, and the next owner's opportunity. Every slide has a claim and a linked proof source.

**ASHA:** The return sensitivity varies at least four things: ARR, exit multiple, net debt, and timing. Show enterprise value and equity value separately. Show MOIC and IRR together. Add a downside where AI ARR contracts, Beacon retention weakens, and the buyer uses a lower multiple. Do not let one base-case cell make the decision.

**COLE:** Then perform the most useful exit-readiness drill before you are selling. Pick your five most valuable claims. Ask whether an independent buyer could verify each one in forty-eight hours without asking the person who built the spreadsheet to explain it. If not, you do not have evidence. You have institutional memory.
