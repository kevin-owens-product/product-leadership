### BUILD THE COUNTRY FILTER BEFORE THE COUNTRY TEAM

**ASHA:** Start with market selection. Vela scores each country on customer pain, reachable account concentration, willingness to pay, incumbent alternatives, partner access, implementation fit, language and workflow localization, regulatory feasibility, support capacity, time to evidence, and capital at risk. Market size is context. Reachable, serviceable demand is the decision unit.

**COLE:** In this fictional pipeline, Germany ranks first because eight named prospects share supplier-exception patterns Vela already supports and two implementation partners have verified access. The Netherlands ranks second with fewer prospects but simpler support coverage. Three other markets have interesting logos and weak implementation evidence. APAC has inbound demand but no validated data, model, support, or channel plan.

**ASHA:** A beachhead is not a flag planted on a map. It is a bounded customer set that reuses enough product and operating capacity to create learning without multiplying exceptions. Vela chooses industrial distributors with high exception volume, existing enterprise-resource-planning connectors, and a clear buyer. It does not begin with every manufacturer, language, and order type.

**COLE:** Willingness to pay is retested because the product has changed. The US offer charges eight dollars per accepted automated resolution under a volume commitment. At one dollar ninety of variable inference and workflow cost, the pre-support contribution is six dollars ten, or about seventy-six percent. That is not Vela's company gross margin; it is the workload contribution before implementation, support, R and D, and company overhead.

**ASHA:** Under the initial European design, variable cost is four dollars thirty-seven before the extra human-review burden created by the quality gap. At the same eight-dollar price, contribution before local support falls to three dollars sixty-three, about forty-five percent. Add launch support, currency, partner expense, and regional fixed cost, and the original case does not clear.

**COLE:** Vela has three economic levers. Reprice for the regional service and customer value. Reroute only work whose data terms permit a more efficient approved model. Narrow the workflow so the local model handles the task it performs well, with more complex cases escalated. The design cannot quietly export restricted data to rescue margin.

**ASHA:** Customer interviews support an eleven-dollar-fifty price for a bounded exception-resolution package with local processing and evidence. Narrowing reduces expensive retries and review, bringing variable cost toward four dollars ten. That leaves seven dollars forty of workload contribution, about sixty-four percent, before fixed regional cost. The number is a target under a signed design, not a booked margin.

### THE COUNTRY P AND L

**ASHA:** The country P and L begins with contracted price and realistic live volume. Then variable model, tool, data, cloud, review, payment, and partner cost. That produces workload contribution. Subtract local implementation, support, customer success, sales capacity, security, legal, tax administration, insurance, and regional platform cost. Then show cash timing, not just annual run rate.

**COLE:** Vela's first twelve-month base case is four point two million dollars of live regional ARR, not the eighteen-million pipeline. Variable and partner cost consume one point five million. Local implementation and support cost one point seven. The regional cell, assurance, and localization cost three point eight in the first year. The country case therefore consumes roughly two point eight million before central allocation and working capital.

**ASHA:** In year two, the base case reaches twelve million of regional ARR. At a sixty-two-percent contribution after workload and partner cost, it produces seven point four million. Regional operating cost settles near four point one million, leaving about three point three million before central allocation. That creates a plausible payback path. A delayed ramp, lower price, or persistent quality gap can erase it.

**COLE:** Currency changes both price and cost. Contracts may be denominated in euros while some model and central costs remain in dollars. Finance models transaction exposure, translation, repricing rights, and hedging policy. It does not turn a current exchange rate into a five-year margin. The board sees constant-currency operations and actual reported cash separately.

**ASHA:** Tax and transfer pricing are inputs, not a percentage added at the end. Which entity contracts, employs, invoices, owns or licenses intellectual property, bears risk, and receives service from the parent affects tax, withholding, indirect tax, transfer-pricing support, and cash movement. Qualified advisers structure it. Operators make sure the actual people, systems, rights, and invoices match the structure described.

**COLE:** Working capital can surprise a software team. Local customers may demand longer payment terms, implementation milestones, performance security, or invoice requirements. Partners may be paid before Vela collects. A profitable annual contract can consume cash during launch. The country model includes billings, collection timing, refunds, credits, and trapped or restricted cash where relevant.

**ASHA:** Capacity is country-specific too. A support promise spanning languages and time zones needs staffed coverage, not a global slide. Implementation partners require certification and escalation. Security and privacy incidents need local clocks and customer contacts. Lina identifies one accountable regional general manager only after the product, control, and P and L gates exist.

### THREE LEDGERS, NOT ONE

**COLE:** The regional general manager operates three ledgers. The commercial ledger moves named demand through contract, implementation, activation, usage, renewal, and expansion. The control ledger moves each obligation through design, evidence, approval, monitoring, and incident response. The financial ledger moves price and volume through contribution, operating cost, cash, and payback. A country is ready only when the same customer clears all three.

**ASHA:** This prevents a common expansion illusion. Sales may count the German parent while implementation discovers five subsidiaries, three data terms, and two unsupported connectors. Legal may approve a transfer pattern that the customer's contract still forbids. Product may pass the generic eval while local documents drive exceptions. Finance may show margin before the support team exists. Each function can be locally correct and the launch globally false.

**COLE:** Vela gives every design customer one entry record joining buyer, contracting entity, workflow, volume, price, currency, implementation owner, data map, model route, support promise, control classification, contribution, cash date, and launch gate. If the record fragments into separate systems, the monthly review reconciles them. A country strategy that cannot be inspected customer by customer is still a market thesis.

### RESIDENCY, TRANSFER, AND SOVEREIGNTY

**COLE:** Data residency asks where data is stored or processed. Data-transfer rules ask whether and on what basis data may move between jurisdictions or entities. Sovereignty is broader: who can control, access, compel, operate, update, or discontinue the service under applicable law and vendor dependency. One regional cloud address does not answer all three.

**ASHA:** Vela's customer contracts add their own layer. A contract may prohibit export even where a legal transfer mechanism could exist. It may require a named subprocessor, approval before model changes, deletion on termination, or evidence of regional support access. The strictest operational constraint may come from the promise Vela sold, not a statute.

**COLE:** The regional architecture has one EU cell. Customer raw data, tenant identity, retrieval, workflow state, tool execution, and logs remain inside that cell under its defined policy. Models execute through approved regional endpoints. Only purpose-limited aggregate operating metrics cross to Vela's corporate view. The common policy and eval contract travels; customer payload does not.

**ASHA:** Common does not mean identical thresholds. The eval schema is shared so evidence is comparable. German language, supplier-document, and local workflow test sets are specific. The service envelope states quality, latency, cost, review, authority, fallback, and incident path for that cell. A US pass cannot certify an EU task.

**COLE:** Model and vendor availability is a design constraint. A vendor may not offer the same model, data terms, logging, support, or capacity in every region. Vela maintains approved routes, performance evidence, contractual terms, dependency concentration, and an exit test. Model sovereignty does not require owning a foundation model. It requires knowing which critical promises depend on a vendor decision Vela cannot control.

**ASHA:** Local execution also limits blast radius. A regional failure can be isolated. The trade is duplicated infrastructure, slower feature rollout, and more operational surfaces. The cell earns its complexity only if customer rights, regulation, reliability, or economics require it. Architecture is a priced response to constraints, not a map-coloring exercise.

### THE REGULATORY OPERATING MAP

**ASHA:** Date the map. As of August seventh, 2026, the European Commission's AI Act framework page was updated on August third. Most of the Act and Article 50 transparency obligations became applicable on August second, 2026. Prohibitions one through eight had applied since February 2025; the Omnibus's new ninth prohibition applies in December 2026. Governance and general-purpose-model provisions had applied since August 2025.

**COLE:** A July 2026 Omnibus changed parts of the rollout. The Commission's current materials place Annex III high-risk timing at December second, 2027 and certain Annex I regulated-product systems at August second, 2028. It also changed the earlier treatment of AI literacy. Any script, policy, or board pack using the pre-Omnibus timeline needs refreshing.

**ASHA:** Article 50 includes transparency duties for certain direct AI interactions and generated or manipulated content, with context and exceptions that matter. The Commission's FAQ was updated July twenty-fourth, 2026. A limited transition for some marking and detection capability on systems placed before August second runs to December second, 2026. Vela does not reduce this to put an AI badge on everything.

**COLE:** Instead, counsel and the product team classify the actual use. Who is provider and deployer? What task does the system perform? Does it interact directly with a person? Does it generate content in scope? Is the workflow listed or connected to a regulated product? What human decision follows? Which records, instructions, monitoring, transparency, or conformity obligations apply and when?

**ASHA:** Vela's bounded order-exception workflow is not declared high risk merely because it uses AI, nor exempted merely because it serves business operations. Its facts must be classified. Non-EU providers can still fall within scope when system outputs are used in the EU. The operating plan keeps a source date, legal owner, system inventory, classification rationale, obligations, evidence, and change trigger.

**COLE:** The EU Data Act has applied since September twelfth, 2025. It can give users of connected products and related services rights to access generated data and metadata and supports switching and interoperability. It does not cancel GDPR, contract, security, trade-secret, or intellectual-property constraints. Vela maps whether supplier and equipment data are received from customers, generated by its own service, or accessed through a third party.

**ASHA:** Controller and processor labels are not slogans either. Roles can differ by data and purpose. Customer instructions, Vela's independent product purposes, support access, security monitoring, model improvement, and partner use need separate analysis. The contract, data flow, product behavior, and record of processing must agree.

**COLE:** Regulatory readiness is therefore a product requirement and an operating capacity. It has owners, backlog, test evidence, release gates, monitoring, and incident response. Legal signs the classification; product and engineering make the classified system real; operations prove it remains real after model, vendor, data, or workflow change.

### CONFLICTING MARKET EVIDENCE

**ASHA:** The OECD reported on January twenty-eighth, 2026 that across countries with comparable data, firm AI use rose from fourteen point two percent in 2024 to twenty point two percent in 2025. Large-firm adoption was fifty-two percent versus seventeen point four for small firms. That supports demand and warns about implementation disparity. Definitions and country coverage vary; it is not Vela's market forecast.

**COLE:** The World Bank's Digital Progress and Trends Report, published in January 2026, offers four useful foundations: connectivity, compute, context and data, and competency and skills. A country can have interested customers and still fail on affordable compute, usable local data, or implementation skill. Those constraints belong in entry economics.

**ASHA:** The external evidence says adoption is rising and uneven. Vela's customer evidence says European demand is real and executable demand is much smaller than pipeline. The technical evidence says a regional cell is possible and currently weaker and more expensive. The legal map says classification and obligations are use-specific and moving. That conflict is the decision environment, not a reason to wait for certainty.

### THREE CREDIBLE CHOICES

**ASHA:** Choice one is one global stack. Keep the US model route, common release train, and central support. Negotiate customer permission for necessary transfers and offer contractual controls. This preserves product velocity and gross margin, avoids infrastructure duplication, and can make Vela globally consistent.

**COLE:** Credible where transfers, customer terms, model availability, latency, and obligations permit it. Here several priority customers prohibit the assumed export, and the incident has raised trust sensitivity. Value potential is high; cash consumption is low; regulatory and contract risk are high; evidence can arrive quickly; reversal becomes painful after customers and data depend on the route.

**ASHA:** Choice two is independent country forks. Separate code, model, data, release, operations, and local P and L for each market. Country leaders can optimize to law, language, partners, and customer demand. A failure stays local. Some regulated businesses deliberately accept this cost.

**COLE:** Also credible for genuinely different products or obligations. For Vela now, it multiplies engineering, evals, patches, security, and support before demand is proven. Quality may drift across forks and customers lose a coherent roadmap. Cash consumption is high; local control is high; speed falls after the first launch; reversibility is low once forks diverge.

**ASHA:** Choice three is one gated EU regional cell. Share product contracts, policy schema, eval methods, and release intent; keep regional data, model execution, identity, logs, support paths, and kill authority inside the cell. Launch Germany first. Add the Netherlands only after the first gates hold. Defer APAC until its own customer, control, and contribution case exists.

**COLE:** This preserves a common product without pretending infrastructure and economics are uniform. It spends before the base clears, and the cell could become an expensive quasi-fork. Value is high if the beachhead converts. Cash use is bounded. Risk is explicit. Evidence arrives by cohort. Reversibility is medium because Vela can stop the cell without corrupting the US service.

**COLE:** Confidence is medium on German demand, medium-low on first-year implementation pace, medium on repricing, and high that APAC is not ready. The company reflects those differences in hiring and forecast. One regional architect may be justified now. A five-country sales organization is not.

### OPERATE THE CELL

**COLE:** The release process uses a common intent and regional evidence. A feature proposal states customer job, data purpose, action, service target, and policy. Each cell implements behind the same interface. Regional evals run before release. If one cell fails, that region holds while others continue. Product does not lower the common promise to make a dashboard green.

**ASHA:** The localization backlog has four classes. Customer language and workflow. System and data. Regulatory and contractual control. Go-to-market and service. Every item has reusable core, local delta, owner, dependency, evidence, and ongoing maintenance. Translation is one row, not the backlog.

**COLE:** Country support has an error budget too. Partner implementation defects, model exceptions, data connector failures, and customer questions consume capacity. The first cohort uses a fixed admission schedule. Sales cannot pull the next customer forward because contract signature exists. A live customer must finish the evidence path before capacity is declared reusable.

**ASHA:** Model routing is reviewed as economics and risk. If a better regional model appears, Vela reruns quality, cost, latency, privacy, logging, security, and action tests. A lower token price does not justify a model change whose error mix increases expensive human handling. A quality gain is not free if vendor terms compromise customer rights.

**COLE:** The monthly country review reconciles pipeline to bookings to live ARR, price and volume to workload contribution, P and L to cash, and control status to release. It also compares Germany with the original US pattern without calling the US the truth. Differences may reflect market, product, customer mix, or measurement. The review names which.
