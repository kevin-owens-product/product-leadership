### BOARD PACK: THE POSITIVE NUMBER THAT ISN'T THE ANSWER

**ASHA:** Six cards. Card one: end-of-quarter ARR, ninety-seven point five million, up from the verified ninety-six at entry. Card two: quarterly revenue, twenty-five point four nine million. Card three: trailing NRR, one hundred percent, down from one hundred and one. Card four: quarterly EBITDA, six point three seven million, a twenty-five percent margin. Card five: net debt, one hundred and fifty-six million. Card six: median onboarding, one hundred and twelve days, with two and a half million of signed contracts still not live.

**COLE:** The apparently positive number is ninety-seven point five million of ARR. It increased by one and a half million. But ending ARR cannot tell you whether the installed base is strengthening. New logos can outrun churn for a quarter while the customer base underneath them decays. Nor does ARR tell you whether revenue was recognized, invoices were sent, or cash was collected.

**ASHA:** This quarter's EBITDA margin is twenty-five percent, below the verified twenty-six point five percent last-twelve-month margin. That may be acceptable if investment is repairing the value drivers. It is not acceptable if the company cannot explain the movement. A board does not need every journal entry. It does need one bridge from customer activity to recurring revenue, recognized revenue, gross profit, EBITDA, cash, and debt.

**COLE:** Product telemetry adds the uncomfortable context. Customers activated in under sixty days have materially stronger retention and expansion than customers taking more than one hundred and twenty days. Harborline's two groups are similar enough in size to compare. The faster cohort runs at roughly ninety-four percent GRR and one hundred and ten percent NRR after a year. The slower cohort runs near eighty-one percent GRR and ninety-two percent NRR.

**ASHA:** Average those cohorts carelessly and you get about eighty-eight percent GRR and one hundred and one percent NRR, close to the verified company baseline. The aggregate is mathematically correct and operationally useless. It hides a controllable driver: activation speed. If you raise prices across both groups, the weak cohort does not become strong. It becomes angry sooner.

**COLE:** That is the adverse fact in this episode. The board wants pricing as a quick value-creation lever. The cohort evidence says a blanket increase before onboarding repair could accelerate churn. Price is not wrong. Sequence is the decision.

### FIVE WORDS THAT SHOULD NEVER SHARE A CELL

**ASHA:** We need a metric contract, but first we need language. Booking, billing, revenue, ARR, and cash are connected. They are not interchangeable. Start with booking.

**COLE:** A booking is the commercial commitment created when a customer signs a contract under the company's booking policy. A three-year contract for three hundred thousand dollars per year may create a nine-hundred-thousand-dollar total contract value booking. It may also create three hundred thousand of annual contract value. Neither number says the service has started, an invoice has gone out, revenue has been recognized, or cash has arrived.

**ASHA:** Billings are invoices issued during a period. If Harborline invoices that customer annually in advance, it may bill three hundred thousand when the service term begins. If it invoices quarterly, billings arrive in smaller pieces. Billings affect accounts receivable and cash timing. They do not determine revenue by themselves.

**COLE:** Revenue is recognized as Harborline satisfies its performance obligations under the accounting policy. For a stand-ready software subscription, that is commonly recognized across the service period. Implementation services may be recognized as work is performed or milestones are met, depending on the obligation. A customer can pay cash before revenue is recognized, creating deferred revenue. It can also consume service before paying, creating receivables.

**ASHA:** ARR is a management metric, not a line in the audited income statement. It annualizes the recurring run rate of qualifying contracts at a point in time. Harborline's contract should count only when the recurring software service has commenced, the customer can use it, the recurring amount is known, and the contract is not a one-time service disguised by repetition. That is our policy choice. Another company may define contracted ARR differently, but it must name the difference and reconcile it.

**COLE:** Cash is what reached the bank. Cash pays interest. A booking does not. An invoice does not until collected. Revenue does not necessarily. ARR certainly does not. When an operator says, "We added three hundred thousand," the next question is: added to what?

**ASHA:** Return to the opening customer. Nine hundred thousand of total contract value is booked. No recurring service has started, so zero enters live ARR under our definition. No invoice has been issued under the assumed start-date billing schedule, so zero billings. Zero revenue is recognized. Zero cash is collected. The commercial achievement is real; its location is backlog, not live recurring revenue.

**COLE:** And that location changes operating behavior. If sales is paid fully at signature while implementation capacity is ignored, the company manufactures backlog faster than value. If ARR includes the contract immediately, leadership sees growth while the customer waits. If customer success does not own the account until go-live, nobody owns the value gap. The metric contract is also an incentive contract.

[PAUSE]

**ASHA:** The seller's one-hundred-million ARR story reconciles cleanly once the categories are separated. Start at one hundred. Remove two point five million of signed-not-live contracts. Remove one point five million of recurring services presented as software ARR. Verified live software ARR is ninety-six million. We have not erased four million dollars of commercial activity. We have put it where it belongs.

### THE ARR BRIDGE: GROWTH HAS INGREDIENTS

**COLE:** Now bridge the first quarter. Opening ARR is ninety-six million. New-logo ARR that actually went live contributes two point eight million. Expansion from existing customers contributes one point four million. Churn removes one point seven million. Contraction removes one million. Ninety-six plus two point eight plus one point four, minus one point seven, minus one, equals ninety-seven point five million of ending ARR.

**ASHA:** That bridge matters because each line has a different owner and remedy. New logos depend on demand, sales execution, implementation capacity, and activation. Expansion depends on adoption and value. Churn may signal product failure, customer failure, competition, budget, or segment mismatch. Contraction often reveals packaging, shelfware, or declining usage before full churn.

**COLE:** Do not try to calculate the reported trailing-twelve-month NRR from one quarterly bridge. The periods and cohorts differ. NRR takes a fixed starting cohort and asks what its recurring value became after churn, contraction, and expansion, excluding new logos. A quarter-end ARR bridge explains the company total. A cohort retention calculation explains the installed base.

**ASHA:** Use a normalized example. Begin with one hundred dollars of ARR from customers present twelve months ago. Churn and contraction remove twelve dollars. The surviving customers expand by thirteen. Ending cohort ARR is one hundred and one. GRR is eighty-eight percent because it ignores the thirteen dollars of expansion: one hundred minus twelve, divided by one hundred. NRR is one hundred and one percent because it includes expansion: one hundred minus twelve plus thirteen, divided by one hundred.

**COLE:** This is why one hundred and one percent NRR can coexist with eighty-eight percent GRR. Expansion is masking twelve points of loss. That may be a valid land-and-expand model if the losses are understood and the expansion is broad. It may be fragile if a few large accounts expand while many smaller customers leave.

**ASHA:** Seller NRR excluded some downgrades and reached one hundred and six. Verified NRR includes them and starts at one hundred and one. By Q1, the trailing figure is one hundred. The direction matters more than the cosmetic threshold. A company can brag about being at one hundred while the cohorts are weakening each quarter.

**COLE:** A good cohort view fixes the population. Group customers by activation quarter, segment, product, geography, or use case. Track starting ARR, surviving ARR before expansion, expansion, and ending ARR over consistent ages. Do not compare a twelve-month-old cohort with a three-month-old cohort and call the difference a trend. Age them to the same point.

**ASHA:** Then connect the cohort to behavior. Harborline's slow-onboarding group has weaker retention. That is correlation, not yet proof that delay causes churn. The segment may be more complex, less technically ready, or sold by a team pursuing poor-fit accounts. The operating response is not to declare causality. It is to test the competing explanations using implementation scope, product usage, support demand, segment, and sales source.

### QUALITY OF EARNINGS: ADJUSTED FOR WHAT?

**ASHA:** The second contested metric is adjusted EBITDA. EBITDA is earnings before interest, tax, depreciation, and amortization. It is a rough measure of operating profit, not cash flow and not intrinsic value. Adjusted EBITDA goes further by removing items management claims do not represent normal ongoing operations.

**COLE:** Some adjustments are legitimate. A one-time transaction fee does not describe the steady-state cost of serving customers. A duplicated public-company cost may genuinely disappear after a take-private. But an adjustment needs evidence that the cost is unusual, nonrecurring, and not required to produce the forecast revenue.

**ASHA:** Harborline's seller case reports twenty-eight million. The verified baseline is twenty-six because the adjustment schedule removes recurring cloud expense and gives credit for savings not yet operationally realized. The exact label does not control the economics. If the cloud service remains necessary next year, restore the cost. If a saving depends on a future action, place it in the value-creation plan with an owner and timing; do not pretend it happened last year.

**COLE:** This is quality of earnings in operator language: how repeatable is the reported profit, how much cash does it convert to, and what customer or operating conditions are required to sustain it? A high-quality dollar of EBITDA comes from recurring revenue with durable retention, clean gross margin, and costs that will not snap back. A low-quality dollar may come from delayed hiring, underinvestment, favorable working capital, or an adjustment that repeats every year.

**ASHA:** The lender and sponsor can also define adjusted EBITDA differently. Debt documents may allow add-backs for projected cost savings subject to caps and time limits. That can increase covenant EBITDA without putting one extra dollar in the bank. An operator needs three reconciled views: GAAP or statutory operating results, management adjusted results, and covenant results. One is not automatically dishonest. They answer different questions and must never be silently substituted.

**COLE:** The red-flag memo should state that Harborline's twenty-eight-million seller EBITDA is not the governing baseline. Twenty-six million is. Any future savings appear in an initiative ledger only after the action, cost, timing, and finance validation are specified. Once realized in actual results, the improvement no longer needs to live forever as an add-back.

### GROSS MARGIN: WHERE DELIVERY HIDES

**COLE:** Harborline's verified last-twelve-month revenue is ninety-eight million and gross margin is seventy-four percent. Multiply them and gross profit is about seventy-two point five million. Cost of revenue is about twenty-five point five million. The margin tells you what remains after the direct costs of delivering the revenue, before product development, sales, general administration, interest, and tax.

**ASHA:** The classification line matters. Hosting required to run the product belongs in cost of revenue. Third-party data or transaction fees tied to usage generally belong there. Customer support and implementation labor may belong wholly or partly there depending on what the company delivers and its accounting policy. Product engineering that builds future capability is usually operating expense rather than cost of revenue. Consistency and economic visibility matter more than flattering placement.

**COLE:** Harborline's recurring services complicate the story. A service contract can recur and still have a labor-heavy gross margin. Calling it ARR does not turn people into software. Separate subscription, implementation, and recurring managed-service revenue. Show the gross margin of each. Then decide whether software and automation can change the delivery model.

**ASHA:** This becomes critical when AI arrives. Model inference, retrieval compute, human review, and exception handling will be direct workload costs. If Harborline sells four dollars of assisted work but spends three dollars delivering it, the ARR can grow while gross margin deteriorates. Episode four will build that workload P and L. Today, the rule is simpler: revenue quality includes the cost required to deliver the promise.

**COLE:** A margin improvement is not automatically value creation. Cutting implementation labor may lift gross margin this quarter and extend onboarding from one hundred and twelve to one hundred and fifty days. That can reduce activation, retention, and future revenue. The full driver tree must connect margin action to customer consequence.

### CAC PAYBACK AND CONCENTRATION: USEFUL, EASY TO ABUSE

**ASHA:** Harborline's verified CAC payback is twenty-two months. Explain the formula before the benchmark.

**COLE:** CAC payback estimates how many months of new-customer gross profit are required to recover the fully loaded sales and marketing cost of acquiring that customer. With an eighty-thousand-dollar average ACV and seventy-four percent gross margin, annual gross profit is about fifty-nine thousand two hundred dollars, or roughly four thousand nine hundred and thirty dollars per month. A twenty-two-month payback implies acquisition cost around one hundred and eight or one hundred and nine thousand dollars.

**ASHA:** That calculation is only as good as the denominator. If the customer takes one hundred and twelve days to activate, when does gross profit begin? If implementation requires substantial services, did you include that cost? If a customer churns before payback, the company never recovers CAC. A single average can hide excellent economics in one segment and destructive economics in another.

**COLE:** So calculate CAC payback by segment, channel, product, and cohort. Use realized gross margin, not the corporate target. Pair it with retention. A thirty-month payback on customers that retain for ten years may be attractive. An eighteen-month payback on customers that leave in fourteen is not.

**ASHA:** Customer concentration works the same way. Harborline's top twenty customers represent twenty-three percent of ARR. That is not automatically safe or dangerous. Examine contract duration, renewal timing, product adoption, switching cost, gross margin, support burden, and expansion. One five-million-dollar account with declining usage can matter more than ten healthy two-hundred-thousand-dollar accounts.

**COLE:** Concentration should also affect product prioritization without controlling it. Losing a large customer hurts. Building bespoke features for every large customer can fragment the product and hurt everyone. The operator needs a transparent exception rule: when does a customer-specific request create reusable capability, and when is it paid custom work?

### METRIC CONTRACTS: ONE NUMBER, ONE DEFINITION, MANY SOURCES

**COLE:** A metric contract is not a glossary entry. It is an operational specification. For ARR, specify the business question, inclusion and exclusion rules, grain, time basis, source systems, owner, refresh cadence, reconciliation, and allowed restatements.

**ASHA:** Harborline's ARR contract joins the signed contract and amendment, service start and end dates, billing schedule, activation telemetry, customer and product identifiers, and general-ledger reconciliation. Billing tells you the commercial schedule. Contract dates tell you the legal term. Activation telemetry tells you whether the service is available and used. The ledger tells you how revenue and receivables were recorded. No single system can answer the whole question.

**COLE:** Identity is the hidden constraint. Three codebases and seventeen schemas mean the same customer may have different IDs, the same site may be duplicated, and an acquired product may define a user differently. If you cannot reliably join account, contract, site, product, and usage, your cohort analysis becomes a story assembled after the result.

**ASHA:** Assign one accountable metric owner, not one owner per source. The CFO should own the financial metric contract. The CRO validates commercial interpretation. Product and data own activation and usage instrumentation. The controller reconciles to the ledger. Disputes are resolved in a logged change process, not by whichever executive presents last.

**COLE:** And preserve history. If the definition changes, recompute prior periods where practical and disclose the restatement. Otherwise a chart can improve because the definition changed. That is not trend analysis; it is a splice.

**ASHA:** A board pack should carry a small definition footer: reported or adjusted, point-in-time or period, currency, cohort, and source date. The full metric contract can live behind it. Precision should reduce debate, not bury the board in metadata.

### BOARD CREDIBILITY IS AN OPERATING ASSET

**COLE:** It is tempting to treat the rebase as a communications problem: find the least alarming wording, pre-wire the directors, and move on. Communication matters, but the deeper issue is decision latency. When directors do not trust the baseline, every request becomes a forensic exercise. A three-million-dollar reliability investment cannot be debated on its merits because the room first spends an hour arguing about whether churn is really churn.

**ASHA:** Trust changes the cost of capital inside the company. A credible management team can bring bad news early, explain the mechanism, and receive permission to act before the damage compounds. A team that has defended convenient numbers needs overwhelming proof for the same request. The formal interest rate does not move, but the practical hurdle rate on every initiative rises.

**COLE:** Credibility does not mean never restating a number. It means making restatement safe. Say what was previously reported, what is now verified, why the definition or source changed, the historical periods affected, and which decisions change. Then keep both the original and restated series available. Erasing the old chart looks cleaner and teaches the organization that truth is editable.

**ASHA:** Nor should the board punish every miss. If management reports ninety-six, later discovers a contract amendment that moves it to ninety-five point eight, and discloses the control failure, that is the measurement system working. If the response is humiliation, executives will wait until uncertainty resolves in their favor. The board must distinguish an honest estimate refined by evidence from a promoted number protected against evidence.

**COLE:** The operator test is simple: can the same baseline support compensation, product priorities, the lender model, and the board conversation without a manual translation layer? If not, Harborline still has several truths. The metric contract is complete only when consequential systems consume it.

### DECISION GATE: WHICH TRUTH GOES TO THE BOARD?

**ASHA:** Choice one: preserve the seller story. Report one hundred million of ARR, one hundred and six percent NRR, and twenty-eight million of adjusted EBITDA while management works to make those numbers true. The case is continuity. A first-quarter rebase can shake employees, lenders, and the board before the new team has had time to operate.

**COLE:** Choice two: quietly adjust internal targets. Keep the promoted numbers in sponsor and board communications, but run product, finance, and compensation from the ninety-six, one hundred and one, and twenty-six baseline. The case is pragmatism. You avoid a public reset while giving operators realistic targets.

**ASHA:** Choice three: rebase publicly. Tell the board the governing baseline is ninety-six million of ARR, one hundred and one percent NRR at entry, and twenty-six million of adjusted EBITDA. Explain the definitions, reconcile the old numbers, update targets and compensation, and build metric contracts. The case is one painful reset instead of permanent dual accounting.

**COLE:** All three have costs. Preserving the story may protect momentum. Quiet rebasing buys time. Public rebasing may look like failure before the team has made a decision. It also gives every future decision a stable denominator.

**ASHA:** Kevin, choose. Then answer this: if sales compensation, lender reporting, and the board pack use different definitions for the next two quarters, which behavior will win?

One and a half million of recurring services moved out of software ARR and remains in the appropriate revenue category. Downgrades returned to NRR. Recurring delivery costs returned to EBITDA.

**ASHA:** The decision memo. Choice: one governing metric system and a public board rebase. Capital: six hundred thousand dollars over twelve weeks for contract reconciliation, identity resolution in the metric layer, activation instrumentation, and finance-data capacity. This is not the larger shared product-data platform from the next episode; it is the minimum control surface required to run the business.

**COLE:** Owner: the CFO is accountable, with named counterparts in revenue operations, product analytics, data engineering, and the controller's team. **ASHA:** Guardrail: no metric-definition change without a documented impact analysis, historical restatement where feasible, and joint approval from finance and the operational data owner. No executive can remove a downgrade, reclassify services, or accelerate ARR through a slide edit.

**COLE:** Kill criterion: if the team cannot reconcile ninety-eight percent of ARR within ninety days, pause blanket pricing action, pause any claim of product-market improvement, and escalate the unresolved population to the audit committee or equivalent board control. If you do not know the denominator, you do not scale the numerator.

**ASHA:** We also change incentives. Sales receives booking credit at signature but a meaningful portion of variable compensation waits for activation. ### OPERATOR DRILL: THE BASELINE PACK

**ASHA:** Your drill has four linked artifacts: a baseline scorecard, ARR bridge, cohort view, and red-flag memo. Start the scorecard with no more than six governing metrics. For each, write the formula, period, population, source, owner, and reconciliation. Put the definition beside the number before debating the target.

**COLE:** Build the ARR bridge from opening to closing. New logo. Expansion. Churn. Contraction. Acquisition or divestiture if relevant. Do not include pipeline. Reconcile the ending balance to contract-level records. Then bridge ARR to revenue so the board can see timing differences rather than treating them as errors.

**ASHA:** Build cohorts with a fixed starting population and consistent age. Show GRR before expansion and NRR after expansion. Segment only where the segment changes a decision: activation speed, customer size, product, use case, channel, or region. Too many cuts produce decorative analytics.

**COLE:** Add behavior to the cohort. Time to first value. Weekly active workflow use. Support burden. Implementation milestones. A retention chart tells you where value disappeared. Telemetry helps you form a testable explanation for why.

**ASHA:** The red-flag memo is one page. List the contested metric, management definition, verified definition, dollar impact, decision impact, and evidence still required. Separate accounting disagreement from economic disagreement. A cost can be correctly classified and still be strategically alarming.

**COLE:** Finish with the board sentence you would be willing to repeat under diligence: "This is the number, this is how it is constructed, this is what changed, and this is what we do because of it." If you need four adjectives before the number, keep reconciling.
